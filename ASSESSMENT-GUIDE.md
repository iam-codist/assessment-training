# Prepare for your Accenture assessment

Start with a small diagnostic, practise the reasoning behind your mistakes, and attempt a timed set when your fundamentals feel steady. This community collection supports independent preparation for India campus technology roles. Your invitation determines the actual stages, languages, timing, navigation and scoring rules.

## Choose your level

Difficulty labels describe the reasoning needed in this library. They are editorial learning levels, not company scores or a judgement of your ability.

- **Easy** applies one concept or follows a short trace. Start here when learning a topic.
- **Medium** combines familiar steps or requires handling an edge case. Explain why an alternative answer fails.
- **Hard** needs several constraints, a subtle interaction or a more advanced algorithm. Work through a small example before timing yourself.

Try five Easy questions in a weak topic. If you can explain four correctly without opening the answers, try three Medium questions. Return to the underlying concept when the same mistake repeats. Attempt one Hard extension after a successful Medium session. These are study checkpoints, not hiring cutoffs. Topic lists progress from Easy to Hard, and search lets you filter a level across learning areas.

## A focused seven-session path

Each session can fit about 60–90 minutes. Stop to review the explanation before collecting more questions. Keep required algorithm practice to at most four problems a day.

| Session | Core work | Finish when you can |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Five pseudocode traces; arrays, conditions and loops | Write a correct variable trace without guessing |
| 2 | Networking, cloud and security basics; five MCQs per topic | Explain the key clue in a scenario |
| 3 | Excel references, Office tools, OS, keys and OOP | Distinguish similar concepts using an example |
| 4 | One array scan, one string problem, one counting problem | State inputs, edge cases and complexity |
| 5 | A two-table join, a three-table join and a grouped report | Predict row counts before calculating totals |
| 6 | Quantity selector, live filter and one cognitive session | Test empty input, limits and user interactions |
| 7 | One mock; review every missed or flagged answer | Write one correction rule for each recurring mistake |

Spend ten minutes each day speaking or writing about what you learned. Use accurate examples from your own projects. For a shorter deadline, attempt a diagnostic set first and spend the remaining sessions on its two weakest topics.

## Manage the clock

For a 45-minute MCQ practice round, use roughly 25 minutes for questions you can resolve, 15 for marked questions, and 5 to review unanswered items. Use this two-pass approach when navigation is allowed. When a test locks previous questions, follow its navigation rules and budget time per item. Read negative-marking instructions before choosing how to handle uncertainty.

For a 60-minute coding rehearsal, spend 5 minutes reading all tasks, 20 on an algorithm problem, 15 on SQL, 15 on frontend and 5 on final checks. Move time to your strongest opportunity when appropriate. Before optimizing, make the basic solution run and test one boundary case. Use only languages listed in your invitation.

During a cognitive game, read the demonstration and objective before acting. Establish the rule first, then speed up. Use the tutorial to learn what resets, what stays visible, and what counts as a completed move.

## Assessment tips by area

- **MCQs** — Underline the condition that changes the answer, such as “at least”, “after grouping” or “no matching rows”. Eliminate choices using a concrete counterexample.
- **Pseudocode** — Record starting values, loop endpoints, indexing, integer division and exactly which loop a break affects. Trace the final iteration explicitly.
- **Coding** — Check empty or single-item input, duplicates, all-negative values, large totals and off-by-one boundaries when permitted by the constraints. Print only the requested output.
- **SQL** — Say what one result row represents. Check join multiplicity, COUNT(*) versus COUNT(column), WHERE versus HAVING, and the effect of a filter after LEFT JOIN. Equal-valued orders are a useful duplicate-total test.
- **Frontend** — Read the existing IDs and TODOs before editing. Check input.value, textContent, listeners and button type. Test typing, clearing, repeated clicks, Enter, and both limits. Keep labels and keyboard operation usable.
- **Communication** — Give the main point, supporting facts and next action. Listen for dates, quantities and owners. Use a comfortable speaking pace and your natural voice.
- **Behavioural and interview** — Use a truthful situation, your responsibility, your actions and the result. Explain uncertainty and tradeoffs. Avoid inventing achievements or trying to guess an ideal personality profile.

## Prepare calmly and recover from a difficult question

The evening before, check your invitation, login, identification requirements and allowed equipment. Complete any required system check. Set a stopping time for revision so you can rest.

Before starting, test audio and connectivity, settle your workspace and read the instructions. If you need an accommodation, contact the recruiting team early. Accenture lists recruiting contacts as the route for adjustments and assessment invitations as the source of technical-support details. [Recruiting FAQ](https://www.accenture.com/in-en/careers/explore-careers/area-of-interest/journey-to-accenture).

When you feel stuck, pause briefly, take a comfortable breath and write down the next fact you can establish. Trace one input or eliminate one option. If navigation permits, mark the item and move on after your time budget. A difficult question gives you one problem to solve; keep attention on the next action you control.

If a technical issue occurs, note its time and error, use the support route in the invitation, and follow the assessment instructions. After the test, record what to practise next. Wait for official results rather than estimating a hiring decision from one question.

## Review before your next mock

Use the topic score breakdown to pick the next practice topic. Log the question ID, your mistake, the correct rule and one example you will retry tomorrow. Reattempt after a delay, with the explanation closed. A copied solution counts as a worked example; an independently solved variation shows you can apply it.
